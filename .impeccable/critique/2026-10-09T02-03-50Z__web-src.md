## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** clearly made for this family, not a generic calendar. The face-picker sign-in, Week rows tinted by person, dashed sticker tiles that stamp a ★, 🌙 "sleeps" countdowns, "Sleepy time" with a giant 😴, and the bedside-amber night clock are all specific to Homebase. The People-Own-the-Color and Fredoka/Figtree rules hold almost everywhere. The specificity breaks in one cross-cutting system: the **nudge banner**. It's a generic white toast with a paragraph of small text, and it looks the same on a parent's phone, over a pre-reader's job tiles, and over the black night screen. The second weakness is density: the heads-up strip and the one long Settings page read like an admin tool laid over the fridge chart.

**Deterministic scan:** `impeccable detect` found 112 items in `web/src`:
- **85 font-size, 13 color and 8 radius advisories.** Most are false positives, because DESIGN.md documents ranges rather than single steps, and black is intentional on the night screen and for darkening person colors. The real drift is a few one-off sizes (.66, .78, .84, .98rem, plus odd px sizes) and the 8px and 16px radii.
- **3 bounce easings.** False positives: the stamp and pop overshoot is the documented kid and celebration play.
- **2 side-stripe borders.** `.agenda-row` uses a person-color stripe, which is borderline but intentional. `.nudge` uses a **Focus Blue** stripe for decoration, which breaks DESIGN.md's rule that Focus Blue is never decoration.
- **1 layout transition.** The progress-bar fill animates `width` (styles.css:173). Minor.

## Overall Impression

The kid-facing heart (tiles, stamps, sleeps, Sleepy time) is genuinely good and matches the Sticker Chart. The biggest opportunity is making the **nudge system context-aware**. It's the one element that shows up on every screen, and it's the one that ignores night mode, kid mode and phone size.

## What's Working

1. **Week grid with people down the side.** Person-tinted rows, emoji categories, and "⚠ Overlap" / "🚗 Needs a ride" drawn as 2px rings with pills. Conflicts line up visually, and the grid stays orderly without looking corporate.
2. **Sticker-chart tiles.** Real `<button aria-pressed>` elements with visible text, an emoji and a dashed person-colored border. They go solid with a ★ when done. A pre-reader and a screen reader can both use them.
3. **The quick-add preview.** "Soccer Thursday 5-6pm Emma weekly" shows What ⚽ Soccer / When / Repeats Weekly, with Emma and Sports already picked. You see what will be saved before you save it.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The nudge banner ignores where it is: night, ambient, kid mode, phone**
- **Why it matters:** it's a full-white card with about 6 lines of small text, and it's the brightest object in a dark kitchen at 10 PM. In kid mode it covers Emma's "Get ready" tiles. On a phone it fills about 40% of the screen and collides with the + button. It breaks night mode, "Nudge, don't nag" and "Kids are real users".
- **Fix:**
  - Collapse it to a one-line title plus "Got it", and tap to expand the details.
  - Use bedside amber on black when night or ambient is on.
  - On kid screens, show only a small parent-facing chip.
  - On phones, cap its height and keep it clear of the + button.
  - Replace the Focus Blue stripe with a status color or an icon.
  - Files: nudges.tsx:31-44 and styles.css:198-209.
- **Suggested command:** /impeccable adapt (with /impeccable quieter)

**[P1] Anyone at the hub can acknowledge a parent's nudge, and that cancels escalation**
- **Why it matters:** `POST /api/nudges/:id/ack` only checks `requireAuth` (server/src/routes/nudges.ts:84). A 5-year-old tapping the biggest navy button on screen silently switches off the "tell the other parent" safety net for a leave-by.
- **Fix:**
  - On a hub session, require the parent unlock to acknowledge leave-by and other urgent nudges, or hide "Got it" in kid mode.
  - Show who acknowledged it.
- **Suggested command:** /impeccable harden

**[P1] Kid mode isn't a space of its own**
- **Why it matters:** the full parent top bar stays in place (App.tsx:113-131): Today, Week, Month and Ambient tabs, ⚙️, Parent unlock and Hub, all as text. A pre-reader can wander into Settings or the PIN pad. Labels such as "RIGHT NOW", "Get ready" and "Tomorrow" have no picture cue.
- **Fix:**
  - On `/kid*`, replace the bar with the face picker plus one way out for grown-ups.
  - Give every kid label a picture (🌙 for Tomorrow, for example).
- **Suggested command:** /impeccable distill (then /impeccable adapt)

**[P2] Today's heads-up strip and the quiet-hours hub layout**
- **Why it matters:**
  - Four heads-up cards of equal weight, each written as "·"-joined prose up to 8 lines long. The real priority ("3 rides still need a driver") reads as a block of text.
  - Titles are forced to lowercase (Today.tsx:71 and :84, Person.tsx:62).
  - At night the left column is an almost empty "Next 6 hours" panel next to a 1,700px right column.
- **Fix:**
  - Make each alert one headline plus a count that opens the list.
  - Stop lowercasing titles.
  - Let "Coming up" or Jobs fill the left column when the timeline is empty.
- **Suggested command:** /impeccable clarify (with /impeccable layout)

**[P2] Phone shell and parent screens**
- **Why it matters:**
  - The phone header wraps to three rows, about 170px of chrome.
  - ⚙️ and 🔔 render as faint grey.
  - On a phone, Week loses the person face as you scroll sideways and shows the hint "drag · double-click", which doesn't apply to touch.
  - Settings is one unsectioned ~4,700px scroll that puts Bills (weekly use) next to Backups.
  - Emma's page heading reads "THU, OCT 15 · OCT 15" (Person.tsx:116).
- **Fix:**
  - Put the icons on the brand row.
  - Make the hint touch-aware.
  - Keep the person column fixed while the grid scrolls.
  - Add section anchors to Settings and move Bills out of it.
  - Remove the repeated date.
- **Suggested command:** /impeccable adapt (then /impeccable polish)

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (parent on a phone, one-handed):**
- The nudge covers 40% of the screen, its "Got it" sits mid-left out of thumb reach, and it overlaps the + button.
- Three rows of header push Today's first item below the clock and the faces.
- The "Assign" action on the ride alert comes after a paragraph of reading.
- Bills are buried in a long Settings scroll.

**Sam (keyboard and screen reader):**
- The nudge region is `aria-live="polite"` with a ~6-line body, so the whole paragraph is read out on every change.
- "🏡 Hub" is a non-interactive `span` styled as a button.
- Month dots carry people by color alone, so each day button's label needs to name them.

**Emma, 5, can't read, on the kitchen tablet:**
- The text nav above her face leads to Week, Settings and the PIN pad.
- At bedtime a text banner covers her Get ready tiles, and its navy "Got it" is the most tappable thing on screen. Tapping it cancels Dad's escalation.
- 9 🌙 and 10 🌙 can't be told apart without counting.
- "Tomorrow" on a packing tile is text only.

## Questions to Consider

- If the hub is "glance, don't study", should a nudge on the shared tablet ever contain a sentence, or just an icon, a face and a "Got it" that needs a parent?
- Kid mode is built inside the parent app's shell. What would it look like as its own space, with no words anywhere and one way out?
- At 10 PM the hub shows four admin alerts and an empty timeline. Should the evening hub become a "tomorrow" board (packing as tiles, the first leave-by time) instead of an empty Today?
